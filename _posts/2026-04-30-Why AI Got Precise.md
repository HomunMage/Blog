Have you noticed that the "it has no idea what I'm asking" frustration of using GPT-4 or Claude has mostly faded?

Especially lately, everyone's saying Claude does tasks more cleanly than GPT, Midjourney churns out cinema-grade images from a few words, while early Stable Diffusion looked like a broken inkjet printer. We call this "more precise" — but if you stop and think about it: **what is this "precision," precisely?**

The philosophical logic underneath is actually pretty interesting. The evolutionary story of AI is, at its core, a process of **socialization**.

### 1. Early AI Was a Weirdo

Remember what AI felt like when you first played with it? Like a strange person who had read every book in the world but had never left the house.

Talented, but it talked weird. Ask it a question and you might get a philosophy essay, or you might get something completely off the rails. Like the "weird people" we know in real life — their internal logic is self-consistent, but it doesn't sync up with the mainstream "frequency."

Early AI didn't feel imprecise because it was dumb. It felt imprecise because it was **too free**. Its world was full of noise, and it didn't know which words you were supposed to say or which aesthetic was the human default.

### 2. "Precision" is Just Learning the Unspoken Rules

Why does Claude or GPT-4 feel precise now?

Because engineers have poured enormous effort into "alignment." In plainer terms: **socialization training**.

They paid thousands of humans to tell the AI: "Hey, no human would understand that sentence," "this image looks ugly to humans," "we usually handle this kind of situation like that."

So when you feel AI has gotten precise, what's actually happening is: **it's finally learned the lowest common denominator of human society.**

*   **Why does Claude feel precise?** Because it's been trained to be very well-behaved. It knows the standard "SOP" for finishing a task and stops wandering off-topic.
*   **Why does Midjourney feel precise?** Because it has a built-in "mainstream aesthetic prior." It knows humans like layered lighting, cinematic feel, golden ratios. Even when your prompt is vague, it auto-fills "the way humans like it."

### 3. The Cost of Precision: It Became a "Senior Civil Servant"

But there's an interesting side effect.

If "precise" equals "socialized," it means AI is becoming more and more "agreeable" — and also more and more "boring."

Look at Stable Diffusion. It's hard to control, it goes wrong often, but if you really know it, it can produce things Midjourney could never produce — work full of personal style and strange beauty.

And Midjourney? It's so precise that one glance and you know "this is an MJ image." It hits the human aesthetic average dead-on, but it's lost that off-the-rails surprise.

It's like the elites in society: well-spoken, accurate at their work, never wrong — and yet you always feel they lack the raw creative energy that only weirdos have.

### Wrap-up

So when we marvel at how precise AI has become, what we're really marveling at is: **"Wow, this thing is acting more and more like a normal person!"**

It's no longer the alien consciousness bouncing around in latent space. It's a "well-socialized adult" who has learned to read the room, follow social inertia, and know what everyone is thinking.

**"Precision" isn't AI finding the truth. It's AI finally learning to perfectly play the role we expected it to play.**

So if AI feels less "fun" now than it used to — don't doubt yourself. It's just that AI has gone from a free spirit to a deeply socialized adult.
